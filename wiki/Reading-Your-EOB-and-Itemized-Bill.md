# How to Read Your EOB and Itemized Bill Side by Side

A summary statement or balance-due notice rarely explains why a number looks high. Comparing your **itemized bill** from the provider against your **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** from your insurer is the fastest way to catch billing or processing errors.

## 1. Get Both Documents Before Paying

- **Itemized bill (from the doctor, clinic, lab, or hospital):** Ask specifically for an itemized statement showing each date of service, procedure/service code, unit count, and billed charge—not a one-line summary balance.
- **Explanation of Benefits (from your health insurer):** Found in your insurer member portal or mailed statement. An EOB is **not a bill**; it shows how your health plan processed the claim.

## 2. Match Each Line Item Across Both Documents

1. **Confirm the date of service and provider name:** Make sure each line matches a visit or test you actually received and check whether the provider was in-network or out-of-network.
2. **Check the units and duplicate charges:** Look for repeated lab panels, extra supply/facility units, or services billed twice on the same date.
3. **Compare allowed amount, plan payment, and patient responsibility:** Check how your insurer calculated your deductible, copay, or coinsurance, and verify whether the provider's billed balance matches what your EOB says you owe.
4. **Flag mismatches for written follow-up:** If the bill asks for more than the EOB shows as patient responsibility—or if a charge looks wrong—ask for a corrected bill or written explanation before paying.
