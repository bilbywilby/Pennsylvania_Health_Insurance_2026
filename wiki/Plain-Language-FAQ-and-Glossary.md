# Plain-Language FAQ and Billing Glossary

Everyday answers to common questions about medical bills, insurer statements, and Pennie coverage—followed by short plain-language definitions of common terms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

- **Is an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) a bill?** No. An EOB shows how your health plan processed a claim (allowed amount, plan payment, deductible, copay, coinsurance, or denial reason). Compare it against the itemized bill from the provider before paying.
- **Should I pay a high or confusing bill right away?** Usually no. Ask for an itemized bill and compare it to your EOB first; if you cannot afford a hospital bill, ask about Financial Assistance (`FAP`) and request a written hold while your request is reviewed.
- **What if an in-network hospital visit or emergency leads to a separate out-of-network bill?** Check your EOB and itemized bill, confirm the service date and facility status, and dispute excess balance billing in writing where federal or Pennsylvania protections apply.
- **What are the key 2027 dates for Pennie?** Open Enrollment runs **November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027**, with **December 15, 2026** as the deadline to pick or renew a plan for **January 1, 2027** coverage.

## Plain-Language Glossary

- **Itemized Bill:** A line-by-line statement from a provider showing dates of service, service/procedure codes, units, and individual charges rather than a single summary total.
- **Explanation of Benefits (EOB):** A statement from your health insurer showing how a claim was processed and what your plan calculated as your patient responsibility.
- **Deductible:** The amount you pay for covered services in a plan year before the plan begins paying its share for those services (subject to plan terms).
- **Copay:** A fixed dollar amount you pay for a covered visit, service, or prescription when required by your plan.
- **Coinsurance:** A percentage of the allowed amount you pay for a covered service, often after meeting your deductible.
- **Out-of-Pocket Maximum:** The yearly cap on your cost sharing (such as deductibles, copays, and coinsurance) for covered in-network care under a plan.
- **Balance Billing:** Charging you the difference between a provider's billed charge and the amount your health plan paid or allowed, where permitted or improperly billed.
- **Financial Assistance Policy (`FAP`):** A hospital program (sometimes called charity care) that may discount or waive eligible hospital bills based on income, household size, and hospital policy.
- **Pennie:** Pennsylvania's official state-based health insurance marketplace for individual and family plans.
