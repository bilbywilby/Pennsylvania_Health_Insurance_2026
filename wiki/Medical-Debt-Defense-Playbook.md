# Medical Debt Defense Playbook

**Step 1 -- Demand debt validation** before admitting or paying anything (FDCPA § 1692g).

**Step 2 -- Compute the statute of limitations**: PA written contracts = 4 years (42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)); tolling/restart events matter.

**Step 3 -- Assert hospital financial assistance** -- if a nonprofit hospital sold this debt without screening for § 501(r) eligibility, that may void collection posture.

**Step 4 -- Respond to any lawsuit** within the answer deadline; failure to respond = default judgment.

**Step 5 -- Post-judgment**: learn which assets are exempt before negotiating.

## What This Means in Real Life (Step-by-Step Example)

Suppose a collection agency calls or sends a letter about a **$2,400** medical bill from several years ago and asks you to "pay $50 today to keep the account active."

1. **Pause before making a partial payment.** Paying a small amount on an old debt can create avoidable disputes over payment history or restart/complicate timelines depending on the facts and date.
2. **Request written validation of the debt** (creditor name, account details, itemized balance, and basis for the amount) and check whether the hospital has a financial assistance policy (`FAP`) that could reduce or wipe out the balance.
3. **Keep a simple log of dates** (date of service, date of first bill/statement, date of any payment, and date of collection notice) so you or a legal aid/consumer attorney can check applicable Pennsylvania time limits (`42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)`) and consumer protection rules.
