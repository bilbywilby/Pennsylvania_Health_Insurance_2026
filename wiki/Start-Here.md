# Start Here: What Happened to You?

Pick the situation closest to yours. Every route leads to plain-language steps — statutes and formal letters come later, only when you need them.

## 🚑 "I got a surprise bill after an ER visit or surgery"

Good news first: a federal law usually protects you here.

- **Emergency care:** You can be treated at an out-of-network emergency room and still owe only your normal in-network copay/coinsurance/deductible. [See the No Surprises Act explained →](Statute-No-Surprises-Act)
- **In-network hospitals:** If an out-of-network specialist (anesthesiologist, radiologist, assistant surgeon, lab) treated you during in-network care, you generally can't be billed more than in-network rates **unless you signed a written waiver**. Check your paperwork before assuming you're stuck.
- **Doesn't count:** Ground ambulance rides are *not* protected by this law. Air ambulances are.
- **Next step:** [[Surprise Bill Playbook|Surprise-Bill-Playbook]], then the ready-to-send [[Template: Act 252 / NSA Rescission Demand|Template-Act-252-NSA-Rescission-Demand]] letter.

## 🔒 "My insurer denied my claim or my appeal failed"

Four steps, in order. Don't skip ahead — each step builds the record the next one needs.

1. **Check the EOB** — that's the "explanation of benefits" sheet from your insurer. It is *not* a bill, but it tells you the reason code for the denial. [Not sure what an EOB is? →](Billing-Jargon-in-Plain-English)
2. **Appeal to your insurer internally** — every denial letter must explain how. Put your appeal in writing and keep a copy.
3. **Complain to the Pennsylvania Insurance Department** — if your plan was bought through an insurer or employer in the usual way ("fully insured"), the state reviews it. Use the [[Template: PID Complaint Narrative (Fully Insured)|Template-PID-Complaint-Narrative-Fully-Insured]].
4. **Independent external review** — after an internal denial on a fully insured plan, an outside reviewer decides, not your insurer. (Self-funded employer plans take the federal ERISA/CMS route instead — [[Statute: PA Act 146 Review|Statute-PA-Act-146-External-Review]] explains which plan type you have.)

Full walk-through: [[Insurer Dispute Playbook|Insurer-Dispute-Playbook]]

## 🏥 "I have hospital bills, a collector is calling, or I'm being threatened with a lawsuit"

Three moves before you pay anything:

- **Ask for an itemized bill first.** Every charge should have a date, a code, and a quantity. Duplicate charges and bundled-up lines are common and fixable. [Red-flag checklist →](Billing-Jargon-in-Plain-English)
- **Screen for hospital financial assistance.** If the hospital is nonprofit, federal law requires it to have a written charity-care policy and to check whether you qualify *before* selling your debt or suing you. Many people qualify and never hear about it. [[Guide: Hospital Financial Assistance|Hospital-Financial-Assistance-Guide]]
- **⚠️ Check Pennsylvania's 4-year clock before paying a dime.** Collectors generally have four years from the breach to sue on a medical debt. A small "good-faith" payment or a signed acknowledgment can **restart that clock**. Compute the timeline first: [[Statute: 42 § 5525(a) SOL|Statute-PA-5525a-Limitations]]

Then: [[Medical Debt Defense Playbook|Medical-Debt-Defense-Playbook]] → [[Template: Debt Validation & Statutory Dispute Notice|Template-Debt-Validation-Statutory-Dispute-Notice]] (make the collector prove the debt exists).
