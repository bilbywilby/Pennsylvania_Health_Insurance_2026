# Plain-English Action Guide: What to Do Step by Step

Health insurance bills and medical collection letters are often confusing. Use this plain-language guide to match your situation to practical next steps.

## 1. You Got a Medical Bill That Seems Too High or Confusing

- **Do not rush to pay** before checking what your insurance plan actually processed.
- **Get two documents:** (a) an **itemized bill** from the provider listing each service, code, and charge, and (b) the **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** from your health insurer.
- **Compare them line by line:** Check that services you did not receive are not listed, units are not multiplied by mistake, and in-network discounts or emergency/out-of-network protections were applied.
- **Next pages:** [[Billing-Jargon-in-Plain-English]], [[Surprise-Bill-Playbook]], and [[PY2027-Marketplace-and-Billing-Overview]].

## 2. You Got a Surprise Bill From an Out-of-Network Provider

- Common examples include an emergency visit or a procedure at an in-network hospital where an outside doctor, anesthesiologist, imaging specialist, or lab bills you separately.
- **Check your EOB and visit records** to confirm the date, facility network status, and whether federal No Surprises Act rules or Pennsylvania network rules apply.
- **Dispute excess balance billing in writing** and attach your EOB rather than accepting a high out-of-network demand at face value.
- **Next pages:** [[Surprise-Bill-Playbook]], [[Statute-No-Surprises-Act]], and [[Statute-PA-Act-252-Network-Disclosure]].

## 3. You Cannot Afford the Bill or Hospital Charges

- **Ask about Hospital Financial Assistance** (often called charity care or a Financial Assistance Policy) directly with the hospital billing department or financial counselor.
- **Ask for a written hold** while your financial assistance application is being reviewed, and keep copies of everything you submit.
- **Avoid putting disputed or hardship medical bills on high-interest credit cards** unless you fully understand the tradeoff.
- **Next pages:** [[Hospital-Financial-Assistance-Guide]], [[Statute-IRS-501r-Financial-Assistance]], and [[Template-Library]].

## 4. A Debt Collector Contacts You About an Old or Disputed Bill

- **Ask for written debt validation** so you have clear records of the creditor, amount claimed, and service background.
- **Be careful before making partial payments** on old or disputed accounts, and keep a timeline of service dates, statements, and letters.
- **Next pages:** [[Medical-Debt-Defense-Playbook]], [[Statute-PA-5525a-Limitations]], and [[Avoid-These-Mistakes]].

## 5. You Are Picking or Renewing a PY2027 Plan on Pennie

- **Mark the 2027 coverage dates:** Open Enrollment runs **November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027**, with **December 15, 2026** as the deadline to select a plan for **January 1, 2027** coverage.
- **Look at your net cost, not just sticker price:** Check renewal notices, tax credit changes, deductibles, and whether your preferred doctors and hospitals are in-network.
