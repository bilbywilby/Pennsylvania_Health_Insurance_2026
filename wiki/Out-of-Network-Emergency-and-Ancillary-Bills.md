# Out-of-Network Emergency and Ancillary Bills: Consumer Checklist

One of the most common billing surprises happens when you choose an **in-network** hospital or go to an **emergency room**, and later get a separate bill from an **out-of-network** clinician or service—such as an emergency doctor, anesthesiologist, radiologist, pathologist, or assistant surgeon.

## 1. Why Separate Bills Happen

- Hospitals often contract with separate physician groups or specialty labs.
- Even when the hospital itself is in-network, an individual specialist involved in your care may bill separately.

## 2. Practical Steps to Take

1. **Pull your EOB and itemized bill together:** Compare the provider name, date of service, and facility listed on the bill against your insurer's EOB.
2. **Check whether federal No Surprises Act rules (`45 CFR Part 149`) or Pennsylvania protections apply:** Covered emergency services and certain out-of-network services at in-network facilities generally limit cost sharing to in-network terms and restrict balance billing.
3. **Do not pay an unexpected excess balance before checking:** Contact your insurer and the billing office in writing if a bill appears to exceed your in-network cost share.
4. **Keep every notice and response:** Save copies of the bill, EOB, claim numbers, letters, and dates so you can escalate to your plan or regulator if needed.
