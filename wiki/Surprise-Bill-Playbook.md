# Surprise Bill Playbook

**Step 1 -- Verify the billing code and network status** of every line item.

**Step 2 -- Apply the No Surprises Act test** (45 CFR § 149): emergency services and certain out-of-network hospital services cannot be balance-billed beyond in-network cost sharing.

**Step 3 -- Send the formal statutory dispute letter** (see Template Library).

**Step 4 -- Escalate on rejection**: provider administrative hold demand -> PA Act 146 external review (fully insured) or CMS complaint (self-funded ERISA).

**Step 5 -- Check the charge against Medicare FMV benchmarks**: <15% of MPFS = pass; 15-40% = audit zone; >40% = presumptive overcharge.

## What This Means in Real Life (Step-by-Step Example)

Imagine you go to an **in-network** hospital for an emergency or scheduled surgery, the facility itself is in-network, and weeks later you get a separate bill from an **out-of-network** anesthesiologist, radiologist, or assistant surgeon.

1. **Match the date of service and provider name** on the surprise bill to your insurer's EOB and your visit records.
2. **Check if your cost share was calculated using in-network rules.** Under federal No Surprises Act rules (`45 CFR Part 149`) and Pennsylvania protections (such as Act 252 where applicable), surprise out-of-network bills in covered scenarios generally cannot exceed your in-network cost-sharing amount.
3. **Send a written dispute before paying extra**, attach the EOB showing the plan's allowed/in-network calculation, and keep copies of every letter, claim number, and call reference.
