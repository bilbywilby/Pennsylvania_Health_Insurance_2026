# What to Do When Your Health Insurance Changes

Changing jobs, income, household size, or coverage programs (such as moving between employer coverage, Pennie, or Medicaid/CHIP) can affect deductibles, networks, and claims still processing from an earlier plan.

## 1. Key Things to Check During a Transition

- **Exact start and end dates:** Know which plan covers each date of service—especially around start dates, end dates, or overlapping bills.
- **Deductible and out-of-pocket resets:** Switching plans often starts a new deductible and out-of-pocket maximum counter, so check how much carried over or reset.
- **Claims for visits before the switch:** A bill arriving weeks later may belong to your *previous* insurer, not your current one.

## 2. Practical Steps to Take

1. **Match the date of service to the active plan on that day** before submitting or reviewing a claim.
2. **Update your insurer and provider billing office** with current coverage details and keep old EOBs from the prior plan.
3. **Save all plan letters, confirmation numbers, and EOBs** in your health folder.
