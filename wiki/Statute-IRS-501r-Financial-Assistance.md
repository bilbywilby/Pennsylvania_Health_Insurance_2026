# IRS § 501(r) — Hospital Financial Assistance

**Plain-language summary:** Section 501(r) of the Internal Revenue Code sets conditions a nonprofit hospital must meet to keep its tax exemption. Practically, it means nonprofit hospitals must (1) establish a **Financial Assistance Policy (FAP)** and make it public, (2) not charge FAP-eligible patients more than **Amounts Generally Billed (AGB)** — the Medicare or insurer rate — for emergency or medically necessary care, (3) make reasonable efforts to determine whether you're FAP-eligible **before** engaging in "extraordinary collection actions" like selling debt, reporting to credit bureaus, or suing, and (4) run a community health needs assessment.

**What to do if a nonprofit hospital is pursuing you:** Invoke § 501(r) with the [[Template: Financial Assistance (IRS 501(r)) Request|Template-Financial-Assistance-IRS-501-r-Request]] letter. If the hospital sold your debt to a collector *without* screening you for FAP eligibility, that sale may violate § 501(r) — a powerful defense position per the [[Medical Debt Defense Playbook|Medical-Debt-Defense-Playbook]].

**Key limits to know:**
- Applies to hospitals recognized as 501(c)(3) — check the hospital's own published FAP first.
- It's a tax-exemption condition, not a private right of action — leverage comes from complaining to the IRS and coupling it with state-law claims.

**Citation block:**
- Statute: 26 U.S.C. § 501(r)
- Regulations: 26 CFR §§ 1.501(r)-1 through 1.501(r)-6
- Notice 69-2 and IRS Schedule H (Form 990) are where hospitals self-report compliance
