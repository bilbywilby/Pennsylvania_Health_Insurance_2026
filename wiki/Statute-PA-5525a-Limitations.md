# 42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a) — Statute of Limitations

**Plain-language summary:** Pennsylvania's four-year statute of limitations governs most medical-debt collection lawsuits: a collector or hospital generally has **four years from breach** to sue on a written contract or obligation. After that window, the claim is time-barred — but it can be *revived* by certain conduct, most notoriously a partial payment, a written acknowledgment of the debt, or an enforceable new promise to pay.

**What this means for you:**
- Before paying anything on an old medical debt, compute the timeline — see [[Medical Debt Defense Playbook|Medical-Debt-Defense-Playbook]].
- Never make a "good-faith" payment or sign anything acknowledging the debt without understanding the restart risk.
- If you're sued on a time-barred debt, that's a defense you raise in your answer — and attempting to collect a time-barred debt can itself violate the FDCPA and FCEUA.

**Key limits to know:**
- Which limitation period applies (4-year vs. others) can depend on how the debt is characterized — plead the characterization issue carefully.
- Tolling exceptions (minority, absence, bankruptcy stay) shift the clock.
