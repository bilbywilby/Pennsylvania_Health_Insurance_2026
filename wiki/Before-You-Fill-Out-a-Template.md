# Before You Fill Out a Template

Our [[Template Library|Template-Library]] letters are powerful — but only when filled out carefully. This walkthrough shows a realistic example so nothing surprises you when you send your first one.

## A Realistic Filled Example

Shown for the Debt Validation letter; the same fields recur across templates.

| Field | Example of what goes here | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| `[SENDER NAME AND ADDRESS]` | Your full legal name and mailing address exactly as they appear on the insurer's or hospital's file | Using a nickname the collector can't match to their records, weakening proof you're the debtor |
| `[DATE]` | The date you physically mail the letter | Dating today but mailing next week — mismatch undercuts your proof |
| `[COLLECTOR/INSURER NAME AND ADDRESS]` | The entity actually communicating with you, from their own letters — *not* the hospital's main address | Addressing the original creditor when a collector is the one calling |
| `[ACCOUNT / CLAIM NUMBER]` | The account or claim number printed on their notices and EOBs | Sending without any account identifier, letting them claim they can't locate you |
| `[DATES OF SERVICE IN DISPUTE]` | The exact dates care was provided, taken from the itemized bill | Saying "around March" — precision signals you keep records and know the file |
| `[AMOUNT DISPUTED]` | The exact dollar figure on the most recent statement | Disputing "everything" without a number — specificity is credibility |
| `[REASON FOR DISPUTE]` | One or two sentences: e.g., "duplicate charges on 03/14/2026; itemized bill requested on [date], never received" | Venting grievances — a dispute letter is a legal instrument, not a diary |
| `[DELIVERY METHOD]` | "Sent via USPS Certified Mail No. 9407 1000 0000 0000 0000 00" | First-class with no tracking — then nothing exists to prove delivery |

## The Delivery Record Is Half Your Power

Almost every right in these letters means nothing unless you can *prove the letter arrived*. Keep, stapled together in one place:

- ✅ A photocopy of the signed, dated letter
- ✅ The certified-mail receipt **and** the green return card (or the online delivery scan, printed)
- ✅ Every EOB, bill, and denial notice received — never discard originals
- ✅ A simple log: date sent, to whom, tracking number, response received, next deadline

## ❌ What Not to Do Yet

- **Don't pay "just to make it go away" while disputing.** A partial payment on an old medical debt can restart Pennsylvania's 4-year collection clock. See [[Statute: 42 § 5525(a) SOL|Statute-PA-5525a-Limitations]].
- **Don't sign payment agreements, hardship plans, or waivers you haven't read in full.** Some hospital forms quietly include admission of liability or arbitration clauses.
- **Don't rely on phone calls.** Verbal promises ("we'll remove that charge") vanish. Follow every phone call with a short written summary and save it.
- **Don't miss a deadline because you were assembling the perfect package.** A timely, imperfect appeal beats a flawless, late one. Calendar every deadline the moment a letter arrives.
- **Don't ignore a lawsuit summons** while working on a strategy — failing to respond converts a fightable claim into an automatic judgment.
