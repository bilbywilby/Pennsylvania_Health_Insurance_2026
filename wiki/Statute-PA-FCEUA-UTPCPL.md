# FCEUA & UTPCPL — Pennsylvania Consumer-Protection Leverage

**Plain-language summary:** Two Pennsylvania statutes give consumers traction against unfair collection and billing practices:

- **FCEUA** (Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act): Pennsylvania's analog to the FDCPA — it prohibits unfair or deceptive collection methods *and* extends coverage to original creditors collecting their own debts, closing a gap the FDCPA leaves open.
- **UTPCPL** (Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law): Pennsylvania's broad consumer-protection statute; deceptive billing practices can support claims for actual damages plus statutory and, in egregious cases, treble damages — historically one of the strongest remedies in this space.

**What to do:** Cite FCEUA in debt-validation and cease-communication letters (see [[Template: Debt Validation & Statutory Dispute Notice|Template-Debt-Validation-Statutory-Dispute-Notice]]) and UTPCPL where a hospital's or collector's conduct crosses from aggressive into deceptive.

**Key limits to know:**
- FDCPA still applies in parallel to third-party collectors.
- UTPCPL treble damages generally require showing knowing/intentional conduct — reserve it for documented patterns, not single billing errors.

**Citation block:**
- FCEUA: 73 P.S. § 2270.1 et seq.
- UTPCPL: 73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq. (treble-damages provision: § 201-9.2)
