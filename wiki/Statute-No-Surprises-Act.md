# No Surprises Act (NSA)

**Plain-language summary:** Effective January 1, 2022, the federal No Surprises Act protects people from most "surprise" out-of-network bills in two big situations: (1) **emergency care** -- you can be treated at an out-of-network emergency room but you only owe in-network cost sharing, and (2) **non-emergency services at in-network facilities** -- if an out-of-network provider (anesthesiologist, radiologist, assistant surgeon, lab) treats you at an in-network hospital, you generally can't be billed beyond in-network rates unless you signed a written consent giving up those protections.

**What to do if you get one:** Send the [[Template: Act 252 / NSA Rescission Demand|Template-Act-252-NSA-Rescission-Demand]] letter, and escalate per the [[Surprise Bill Playbook|Surprise-Bill-Playbook]].

**Key limits to know:**
- Ground ambulances are *not* covered (air ambulances are).
- The protections apply to most employer and marketplace plans, but some grandfathered plans are exempt.
- Providers must still bill your insurer; you can be asked for normal cost sharing only.

**Citation block:**
- Statute: No Surprises Act, Division BB of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (Pub. L. 116-260)
- Core regulations: 45 CFR § 149
- Federal dispute-resolution process: 45 CFR § 149.410 (open negotiation) and § 149.430 (independent dispute resolution)

## In Plain English: How This Protects You

If you have a covered health plan and get emergency care or an out-of-network specialist bill at an in-network hospital, you generally should not be hit with a giant balance bill above your normal in-network cost share. Ask for an itemized bill and EOB, and dispute excess out-of-network charges in writing.
