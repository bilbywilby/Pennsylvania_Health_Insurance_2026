# Deductibles, Copays, Coinsurance, and Out-of-Pocket Maximums in Plain English

Health insurance cost-sharing terms can look similar on statements, but each one works differently when a claim is processed.

## 1. How Each Cost-Sharing Piece Works

- **Deductible:** The amount you pay for covered services before your health plan starts paying its share for those services (some plans cover preventive care or certain visits before the deductible is met).
- **Copay:** A fixed dollar amount (such as $25 or $50) you pay for a covered visit, service, or prescription when required by your plan.
- **Coinsurance:** A percentage of the plan's allowed amount (such as 20%) that you pay after meeting your deductible.
- **Out-of-pocket maximum:** The cap on what you pay for covered in-network services in a plan year (deductibles, copays, and coinsurance typically count toward it; monthly premiums and non-covered charges generally do not).

## 2. Practical Steps When Reviewing a Statement

1. **Check your EOB first:** See whether your insurer applied a deductible, copay, or coinsurance—and check how much of your annual deductible and out-of-pocket maximum have been met so far.
2. **Compare the EOB to the provider bill:** Make sure the bill does not ask for more than your EOB patient responsibility on a covered in-network claim.
3. **Keep track of your year-to-date totals:** Save EOBs across the year so you notice if a claim is processed out of order or a limit is miscalculated.
